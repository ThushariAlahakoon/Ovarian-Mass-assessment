Purpose and scope
This protocol standardises how adnexal masses are scanned, described, risk-stratified
and reported, so that every report carries an IOTA-based description, a validated risk
estimate and a clear management recommendation.

       Applies to: all transvaginal and transabdominal gynaecological ultrasound where an
       adnexal (ovarian, para-ovarian or tubal) lesion is identified, including incidental
       findings and follow-up scans.
       Users: sonographers, sonologists, O&G and MFM specialists, trainees and Western
       Sydney University student sonographers on placement.
       Excludes: acute presentations where O-RADS/IOTA risk models do not apply
       (suspected torsion, ectopic pregnancy, tubo-ovarian abscess/PID) — these are
       reported descriptively with urgent clinical communication; adnexal masses in
       pregnancy are scanned with the same lexicon but risk models are interpreted with
       caution.
       Approach adopted: IOTA terminology for description, a two-step IOTA strategy
       (Simple Rules / Simple Rules risk, then ADNEX for inconclusive or complex lesions),
       with an O-RADS US v2022 category added to every report for referrers and radiology
       interoperability.


Current practice and risk-stratification systems
International practice has moved from CA125-weighted scores (RMI) to ultrasound-based
IOTA models, with the IOTA two-step strategy (modified benign descriptors, then ADNEX)
now the IOTA Steering Committee's recommended approach and O-RADS US v2022 the
common language for risk category and management.

Transvaginal ultrasound by an experienced examiner is the first-line test everywhere.
Subjective assessment by a Level III (expert) examiner performs best; validated models
are recommended when expert review is not available, and to make reports reproducible.

The 2021 ESGO/ISUOG/IOTA/ESGE consensus found the IOTA ADNEX and Simple Rules
risk models outperform the RMI (Grade A). A randomised trial cited there showed IOTA
Simple Rules triage reduced surgery compared with the RMI-based RCOG protocol
without delaying cancer diagnoses.


National and RANZCOG recommendations
Australia has no single national adnexal-mass imaging guideline: ASUM sets scanning
standards and asks for IOTA description, while Cancer Australia and RACGP referral
guidance still use the RMI (>200 = gynaecological oncology referral).

       RANZCOG. No current standalone RANZCOG clinical statement on ultrasound
       characterisation of adnexal masses was found. RANZCOG's O&G Magazine "The
       Ovary" issue (Autumn 2020) describes the IOTA approach — classic lesions, Simple
       Rules and O-RADS — and gyn-oncology referral of suspicious masses (O&G
       Magazine). RANZCOG trainees and Fellows are therefore working to international
       (IOTA/ESGO/RCOG) standards. Open question: confirm with RANZCOG Women's
       Health Committee whether a statement is in development.
       ASUM. The ASUM gynaecological scan guideline lists ovaries and adnexa as
       mandatory structures and directs that cysts and solid masses be described according
       to IOTA; a 2026 edition, Guidelines on the Performance of a Gynaecological Ultrasound
       Examination, is listed on ASUM's standards page (ASUM standards; 2020 draft).
       Cancer Australia. For symptoms persisting over a month, GPs should request a
       transvaginal ultrasound plus serum CA125, calculate the RMI, and refer to a
       gynaecological oncologist when it exceeds 200; CA125 alone should not be used (GP
       card, 2019). The Optimal Care Pathway for women with ovarian cancer sets the
       expectation that confirmed or suspected cases are referred to a gynaecological
       oncologist (OCP quick reference).
       RACGP. The AFP review advises urgent gyn-oncology assessment for RMI >200,
       referral for any IOTA M-rule feature, and discussion with gyn-oncology when CA125
       exceeds about 250 U/mL (AFP 2015).

International guideline recommendations
The European societies recommend expert ultrasound or IOTA ADNEX as the primary
triage tool; the ACR recommends O-RADS; the UK and US national guidelines still
reference RMI and CA125 but have incorporated IOTA Simple Rules.

ESGO/ISUOG/IOTA/ESGE Consensus (2021) — 18 statements (full text):

   1. Subjective assessment by a Level III examiner is the best discriminator (Grade A).
   2. Where no expert is available, ultrasound-based models should be used (B).
   3. IOTA SR risk or ADNEX is preferred over CA125, HE4 or ROMA (B).
   4. ADNEX and SR risk outperform RMI and other scoring systems (A).
   5. ADNEX helps separate benign, borderline, stage I, stage II–IV and secondary
       metastatic tumours (C).
   6. An ADNEX metastatic-tumour risk ≥10% should trigger a search for a primary
       elsewhere (D).
   7. CA125 is the best single biomarker but adds little to ultrasound models; CEA and
       CA19-9 help identify secondary tumours.
   8. MRI (with DCE and DWI) is a second-line test after ultrasound for indeterminate
      masses (B); imaging alone cannot predict peritoneal resectability.
   9. Pathway: physiological finding (O-RADS 1) → discharge or rescan at 6 weeks; <1% (O-
       RADS 2) → conservative follow-up at 3–6–12 months if asymptomatic; 1–<10% (O-
       RADS 3) → general gynaecology; ≥10% (O-RADS 4–5) → gynaecological oncology
       centre.

IOTA updated terms and definitions (April 2026) — the IOTA Steering Committee now
recommends the benign descriptors, ADNEX and the two-step strategy, and the 2026
paper updates definitions users found ambiguous. It maps ADNEX risk directly to O-
RADS: BDs or <1% = O-RADS 2; 1–<10% = O-RADS 3; 10–<50% = O-RADS 4; ≥50% = O-
RADS 5 (UOG 2026; 67:702–724). In the IOTA5 validation, the two-step strategy had an
AUC of 0.94 (Landolfo 2023).

ACR O-RADS US v2022 — six categories (0 incomplete, 1 normal, 2 almost certainly
benign <1%, 3 low risk 1–<10%, 4 intermediate 10–<50%, 5 high ≥50%), with separate pre-
and postmenopausal management. v2022 added the bilocular cyst and acoustic
shadowing in smooth solid lesions as benign features, allows short-interval US follow-up
for O-RADS 3 cystic lesions, keeps gyn-oncology involvement for O-RADS 4 and referral

RCOG — Green-top 62 (premenopausal, 2011) recommends IOTA Simple Rules, referral of
any M-rule mass to gyn-oncology, specialist ultrasound for inconclusive masses, and gyn-
oncology discussion when CA125 >200 U/mL (summary). Green-top 34 (postmenopausal,
2016) uses RMI-I with CA125 for triage. The ROCkeTS study now argues for IOTA ADNEX
at a 10% threshold as the new standard in the UK (BMUS 2025).

NICE — CA125 then abdominopelvic ultrasound for women ≥40 with persistent
symptoms; AFP and β-hCG as well as CA125 for women under 40; CT for staging when
ultrasound, CA125 and clinical status suggest cancer; MRI not routine (NICE summary).

ACOG Practice Bulletin 174 (2016) — gyn-oncology referral for postmenopausal women
with elevated CA125, or for any woman with suspicious ultrasound findings, ascites,
metastatic disease or high IOTA/biomarker risk scores (ObG Project summary).

SOGC Guidelines 403/404 (2020) — initial ultrasound using pattern recognition or IOTA
models, with referral for features suspicious of malignancy (reference list).

Where they agree: TVUS (with transabdominal for large masses) is first-line; describe
with IOTA terms; colour Doppler is part of the assessment; MRI is second-line for
indeterminate masses; suspected malignancy goes to a gynaecological oncologist.

Where they differ: RMI and CA125 still drive Australian GP and UK postmenopausal
pathways, while ESGO/IOTA/ACR place ultrasound models first. This protocol reports
both, with IOTA/O-RADS determining the recommendation.


Scanning protocol
Every adnexal lesion is examined transvaginally (plus transabdominally if large), described
with IOTA terms, measured in three orthogonal planes, scored with colour Doppler, and
then run through the two-step strategy before the patient leaves.

A. Before the scan
   1. Clinical data (mandatory for risk models): age, menopausal status (postmenopausal
       = ≥12 months amenorrhoea, or age ≥50 after hysterectomy), LMP/cycle day, hormonal
       therapy, pregnancy status, symptoms (pain, bloating, early satiety, bleeding),
       personal/family history of ovarian, breast, bowel or endometrial cancer, known
       BRCA/Lynch status, prior adnexal surgery, endometriosis.
   2. Bloods if available: CA125 (date and value); CEA/CA19-9, AFP, β-hCG, LDH as clinically
      indicated. Absence of CA125 does not delay the scan — ADNEX can run without it.
   3. Consent and chaperone for the transvaginal examination per ASUM; offer
       transabdominal/transrectal alternatives where TV is declined or unsuitable.
   4. Bladder: empty for TVUS; partially full for an initial transabdominal survey of large
       masses.


B. Equipment and settings
       High-frequency endovaginal probe (typically 5–9 MHz) and curvilinear abdominal
       probe (2–5 MHz).
       Grey-scale optimised: focus at the lesion, depth so the lesion fills about two-thirds of
       the screen, harmonics on.
       Colour/power Doppler: low pulse repetition frequency (about 0.3–0.6 kHz), low wall
       filter, colour gain raised until noise appears then reduced just below it. Use the same
       settings at follow-up.
       IOTA ADNEX/Simple Rules software on the machine, or the IOTA app/online calculator.


C. Systematic survey
   1. Uterus (size, myometrium, endometrial thickness and pattern — a thick endometrium
       with a solid ovarian mass raises granulosa-cell tumour or synchronous endometrial
       cancer).
   2. Right and left ovary: identify each; measure in three planes; record antral follicles if
      normal.
   3. Locate every lesion: ovarian, para-ovarian, tubal, or uncertain. Use the sliding/split
       sign and pressure with the probe to separate ovary from lesion. Consider mimics
       (pedunculated fibroid, hydrosalpinx, peritoneal inclusion cyst, bowel, pelvic kidney).
   4. Tenderness and mobility of each ovary/lesion ("soft markers"); sliding sign for pouch
       of Douglas obliteration.
   5. Free fluid: pouch of Douglas only (record depth) vs ascites (IOTA: fluid outside the
       pouch of Douglas).

   6. Transabdominal survey of upper abdomen when a lesion is suspicious (O-RADS ≥4 or
       any M feature): ascites in Morison's pouch, omental thickening/cake, peritoneal
       nodules, liver surface, hydronephrosis, pleural effusion.


D. Lesion characterisation (IOTA terms)
For each lesion, record:

   1. Size: three orthogonal diameters of the whole lesion (largest diameter first, in the
      plane where it is longest; rotate 90° for the third).
   2. Tumour type: unilocular · unilocular-solid · multilocular · multilocular-solid · solid (solid
       tissue ≥80% of the lesion on the 2D section). Count locules; record whether >10.
   3. Cyst wall and septa: smooth or irregular (irregularity <3 mm is not a papillary
       projection).
   4. Cyst content echogenicity: anechoic · low-level · ground-glass · haemorrhagic · mixed.
   5. Solid components: presence; maximum diameter of the largest solid component in
       three planes (papillary projections are included as solid components; the largest
       papillation is measured in three planes).
   6. Papillary projections: solid tissue projecting into the cyst cavity with height ≥3 mm.
       Count: 0, 1, 2, 3, >3. Note surface (smooth/irregular) and flow within them.
   7. Solid lesion surface: regular or irregular (O-RADS: smooth vs irregular contour).
   8. Acoustic shadowing: present/absent (a strong benign feature — dermoid, fibroma,
       Brenner).
   9. Colour score of the whole lesion: 1 no flow · 2 minimal · 3 moderate · 4 abundant.
       Record flow in solid components separately.
10. Bilateral: yes/no; if bilateral, characterise and score each lesion separately.
11. Pattern recognition: suggested diagnosis (e.g. endometrioma, dermoid, haemorrhagic
       cyst, cystadenofibroma, hydrosalpinx) and degree of confidence.


E. Risk assessment — apply in order
   1. Normal / physiological? Follicle or corpus luteum ≤3 cm in a premenopausal woman →
       O-RADS 1; stop.
   2. Step 1 — IOTA modified benign descriptors (all require largest diameter <10 cm):
              BD1: unilocular, ground-glass, premenopausal (endometrioma)
              BD2: unilocular, mixed echogenicity with acoustic shadows, premenopausal
              (dermoid)
              BD3: unilocular, anechoic, regular walls
              BD4: any other unilocular cyst with regular walls

       If one applies → almost certainly benign (O-RADS 2).
   3. Step 2 — IOTA ADNEX when no BD applies. Enter: age, CA125 if available, centre type
       (record as "non-oncology centre" unless the patient is scanned in a tertiary gyn-
       oncology unit), maximum lesion diameter, maximum diameter of largest solid
       component, >10 locules, number of papillary projections, acoustic shadows, ascites.
       Record total risk of malignancy and the five subclass probabilities.
   4. Cross-check with IOTA Simple Rules (B1–B5, M1–M5) and record the result — this
       keeps the report RCOG/RACGP-compatible and flags any M feature.
   5. Assign O-RADS US v2022 category from the ADNEX risk band (IOTA 2026 mapping)
       or O-RADS descriptors, whichever is higher. Where pattern recognition by a Level III
       examiner disagrees with the model, state both and explain.
   6. Escalation in clinic: an M feature, ADNEX ≥10%, or ADNEX metastatic risk ≥10% →
       same-day review by the reporting specialist before the patient leaves.


F. Images to store (minimum)
       Each ovary in two planes with measurements.
       Lesion: three orthogonal measurements; the largest solid component measured; each
       papillary projection; any shadowing; the most complex section.
       Colour Doppler image at the most vascular area (with colour score).
       Cine clip sweeping through the whole lesion (recommended for MDT and audit
       review).
       Pouch of Douglas fluid; upper-abdomen images if suspicious.


G. Special situations
       Pregnancy: same lexicon; ADNEX is not yet validated in pregnancy (p-IOTA study
       ongoing) — interpret with caution; decidualised endometriomas can mimic
       malignancy.
       Suspected torsion, ectopic, TOA/PID: report descriptively, do not assign O-RADS;
       urgent verbal communication.
       High-risk patients (BRCA/Lynch): apply the protocol, but lower the threshold for
       specialist referral; O-RADS management may be modified by clinical risk.
       Postmenopausal ovaries not seen: document; transabdominal attempt; state "ovaries
       not visualised" rather than "normal".

Reporting protocol

ADNEX risk), Simple Rules features, an O-RADS v2022 category, a suggested diagnosis
and one explicit management recommendation with a timeframe.

Triage pathway




IOTA two-step triage mapped to O-RADS US v2022 actions

Physiological findings and benign descriptors exit early; everything else gets an ADNEX
risk, and the O-RADS band sets who manages the patient and how fast the referrer is
called.


Quality assurance, training and audit
Diagnostic accuracy depends on examiner expertise, so the protocol is backed by IOTA
certification, double-reporting of higher-risk lesions and an annual audit against histology
and follow-up outcomes.

       Competency: all sonographers and sonologists complete the IOTA online certification
       (or equivalent) before independent reporting; refresh when the IOTA definitions
       change (latest April 2026).

       Expertise levels: ESGO consensus uses EFSUMB levels — Level I ≥300 supervised
       examinations; Level II ≥2000 gynaecological examinations and ≥500/year to maintain;
       Level III expert. O-RADS 3–5 and all inconclusive lesions are reviewed by a Level III
       examiner (MFM/gynaecological sonologist) before the report is finalised.
       Students and trainees (WSU placements): acquire images and complete the IOTA
       worksheet; the supervising sonographer and reporting specialist verify
       measurements and ADNEX inputs.
       Worksheet: an electronic IOTA/O-RADS worksheet in the reporting system so
       mandatory fields (menopausal status, solid component size, papillary count, colour
       score, ascites) cannot be left blank.
       Audit (annual): proportion of reports with complete IOTA fields and an O-RADS
       category (target ≥95%); BD/ADNEX/O-RADS category vs histology or ≥12-month
       follow-up outcome; malignancy rate per O-RADS category vs expected bands; time
       from O-RADS 4–5 report to referrer phone call (target same day); surgery rate for O-
       RADS 2 lesions.
       Review: protocol owner reviews annually and when ACR, IOTA, ASUM or RANZCOG
       publish new guidance; multidisciplinary case review with gyn-oncology for discordant
